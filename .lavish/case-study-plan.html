1 · What's actually being asked

The build

A conversational assistant — CLI, notebook, API, or thin web app — for internal Owners Forum staff to ask about accounts, people, events, activities, relationships, sources and get trustworthy, actionable answers.

The constraint

The dataset is a closed world: 7 linked CSVs, nothing else. No web search, no outside facts. Every claim must trace to a row or source_id — a grounding/trust exercise dressed up as a chatbot exercise.

3 · The eight required behaviors

The real spec — everything else on this page exists to satisfy these eight lines.

1
Answer only from provided files

No outside knowledge, no invented facts.

2
Cite record & source IDs

Every claim traceable to a row.

3
Separate public vs. internal

Never present internal as public fact.

4
Flag weak evidence

Missing, stale, conflicting, low-confidence.

5
Protect restricted contacts

Respect contact_visibility, every time.

6
Ask when ambiguous

Clarify rather than guess.

7
Choose the right response mode

Answer / follow-up / refuse / escalate.

8
Track session vs. dataset

Temp notes stay distinct from CSVs.

4 · Policy rules to enforce

Aligned to the provided policies.md file.

Contact-detail visibility
shareable_businessEmail + phone may be shown
email_onlyEmail shown, phone withheld
restrictedNeither shown — permission required
internal_onlyNot shown externally — internal-only summary
Conflicts

Flag disagreement, cite both sides, prefer newer evidence — don't drop the old.

Sensitive topics

Succession/ownership/family/health → cited + reliability-labeled + human-review flag on rumors.

Ambiguity

Multiple plausible matches → ask, or present options with citations.

5 · Proposed approach

A ~130-row, 7-file closed dataset is a structured retrieval + policy-gated composition problem, not vector-search RAG — embeddings would blur exact-match lookups (names, IDs) a query engine handles perfectly.

flowchart TD
  U["User message"] --> I["Intent & entity resolver<br/>names/IDs, session notes checked first"]
  I -->|ambiguous| C["Clarifying question<br/>present options + citations"]
  I -->|resolved| R["Structured retrieval<br/>joins on account_id / person_id / event_id"]
  R --> P["Policy gate<br/>visibility · sensitivity · conflict check"]
  P -->|clean evidence| G["Grounded answer composer<br/>claim → record_id + source_id"]
  P -->|restricted / rumor| H["Refuse or escalate<br/>human-review flag"]
  P -->|thin / conflicting| F["Answer with caveat"]
  SN[("Session notes store<br/>separate, session_id + timestamp")] -.-> R
  G --> OUT["Response"]
  H --> OUT
  F --> OUT
  C --> OUT
Why not vector RAG

~130 rows fit in memory; deterministic ID joins beat semantic search and keep citations exact.

LLM's job is narrow

Resolves intent/entities, composes prose from retrieved rows — never free-generates facts.

6 · Response-mode decision logic

  • Answer directly
    Clear match, sufficient evidence
  • Ask a follow-up
    Ambiguous or under-specified
  • Refuse
    Restricted, or unsupported claim
  • Escalate to human
    Sensitive rumor / restricted note

8 · Prompts as your test suite

Each prompt exercises a specific behavior from §3 — treat this as acceptance criteria.

Traps planted in the data (verified against the CSVs)

  • Data-center distractors: only Valen truly qualifies as a German family-owned industrial active in data centers (S011, public). LumenGrid isn't family-owned (ACT011), TannenWerk is a low-reliability unverified rumor (S014), Elmbach is exploratory with no confirmed investment (ACT010).
  • Internal-only RSVPs: Meridian's Berlin-dinner confirmations (AT011, AT025) come from internal sources — a correct answer says yes and labels the evidence internal.
  • Hansei has no relationship rows: the "who knows someone at Hansei" prompt has no direct path in relationships.csv; the account is confidential and Jae Park (P019) is contact-restricted — a naive answer invents a connection.
  • Restricted succession notes: Northstar's external-chair note (ACT004) and Hansei's staged handover (ACT005) are confidential/restricted — they must trigger the human-review flag, not casual repetition.
